Dear Pinterest Product Management Team,

I have spent the last few years diving deep into software engineering, refining my
technical skills and building AI-powered applications. But through that process, I
realized something fundamental. My greatest strength, and what I am genuinely excited
about, is not writing the code itself. It is the strategic and creative process of
deciding what to build and why it matters. That realization caused me to pivot from
software development into product management, and it is why I am applying for the
Product Management Apprenticeship at Pinterest.

I took a non-traditional path into tech. Before I earned my associate's degree or wrote
my first script, I was a yoga teacher. In many ways, teaching yoga is a lot like
developing a product. You design the sequence of poses, the words, the music, and the
feeling in the room to deliver specific benefits. It requires constant feedback, clear
communication, and collaboration to ensure the experience helps the students. That same
focus on user experience is what drew me into tech.

Over the past few years, I've built AI-powered applications, designed agentic workflows,
and developed deep fluency in using AI as a creative copilot. But I kept realizing my
strongest contribution wasn't the technical implementation. It was the vision, the
strategy, the creative decisions.

At Revature, I was put in charge of a pilot training program for college seniors. I
wasn't just teaching. I was designing a product. I defined the curriculum, built the
engagement strategy, ran the Discord community, and tracked completion metrics. When
those metrics showed students repeatedly asking a question that was already covered, I
didn't just answer it. I redesigned how the content was presented so they'd actually
consume it. I treated the program like a product: analyze the data, identify friction,
iterate, and measure improvement. That's the work I want to do full-time.

My most recent project, an Agentic Prediction Copilot, reflects my builder mindset and
focus on decision quality. When designing the architecture, I consulted scholarly studies
to evaluate different frameworks. Instead of designing a complex, multi-agent system, the
research led me to a simpler architecture that outperformed the alternatives. The tool is
built around a belief I hold deeply: that the best decisions come from humans and AI
working together, with the AI handling the heavy data processing and the human focusing
purely on high-quality judgment.

Ultimately, I am passionate about guiding the vision of a project from an abstract idea
into a concrete solution that users love. I have the technical skills and AI fluency to
collaborate with engineers from a place of real understanding, but my focus remains
firmly on product strategy and creative problem-solving. I cannot imagine a better place
to start my product management career than at Pinterest, where AI is treated as exactly
what I believe it should be: a partner to human creativity.

Thank you for your consideration.
Sincerely,
Dylan Rigney
